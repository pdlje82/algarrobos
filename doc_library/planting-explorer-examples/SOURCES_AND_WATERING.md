# Planting Explorer: evidence and establishment watering

Research date: 28 October 2026. Scope: the three example posters only. No application values were changed.

## Geometry and botanical decisions

**Algarrobo.** The visible collar and broad, shallow planting hole use general container-tree principles from [University of New Hampshire Extension](https://extension.unh.edu/resource/planting-and-mulching-trees-and-shrubs-fact-sheet): locate the first structural roots, measure the prepared ball, use its height to establish depth, and make the hole wider. The poster's 2–3 × W is general guidance, subject to soil/site assessment; it is not a carob trial result or permission to excavate through unsuitable ground. The [INRGREF/INCREDIBLE carob research factsheet](https://www.nwfps.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Improvement-of-carob-cultivation-techniques-min.pdf), pp. 1–2, documents the young taproot and nursery-induced root problems. Its experimental nursery treatments are not instructions to cut a purchased plant's roots. The root inset communicates a primary root plus laterals, without claiming a standard depth or shape. [UAL's herbarium description](https://www.ual.es/vida-universitaria/patrimonio-cultural/ciencias-naturales/obra/22483/ceratonia-siliqua-l) supports the above-ground identity.

**Palmito.** [UF/IFAS, Transplanting Palms in the Landscape](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/EP001/pdf), PDF pp. 1–3, explains roots arising from the basal root-initiation zone, roughly twice-container-width holes, and the need to judge container-palm depth from the root–shoot interface. The poster therefore does not reuse the tree's exposed root-collar rule. It avoids a fixed depth offset: confirm the actual young plant's interface and nursery planting level. The illustration shows adventitious roots, not a taproot. This is general palm guidance, not a validated *Chamaerops* restoration protocol for Almería.

**Esparto.** [UAL BIOCON](https://nevada.ual.es/biocon/macrochloa-tenacissima.html) supports the identity, tussock habit, persistent basal material and rolled leaves. [University of Idaho Extension, Ornamental Grasses for Idaho Landscapes](https://content-hub.uidaho.edu/api/public/content/93cfd4bb439244638f6cf41419fa5286?v=dab42d56), PDF p. 3, supports retaining a grass's nursery planting depth. The plug illustration is a proposed stock scenario, clearly labelled for confirmation; no plug format was established in NIJAR-003. No fixed pit multiplier, root depth or wild-clump harvesting technique is prescribed.

All three main sections show positioning before backfilling. They do not instruct leaving roots exposed or an open pit around the plant. Backfill, establish soil contact and water as appropriate to the accepted stock and soil. The separate root insets are conceptual; actual nursery root systems will differ.

## Watering: what the evidence establishes

“Minimum” means the least water that still meets a defined survival target; “optimum” additionally needs a target such as growth, survival or water efficiency. None of the reviewed sources establishes those two quantities for these three newly planted nursery stocks on this project's parcel.

| Plant | Published example or guidance | Duration and limits | Minimum / optimum for this project |
|---|---|---|---|
| Algarrobo | A Moroccan field experiment compared 8 L/plant/week through subsurface drip, approximately 30 L/plant/month via tanks, and an unirrigated treatment. | Measurements were on three-year-old trees, originally transplanted at six months. This does not establish a newly planted 1 L seedling's first-summer dose or watering duration. | Unresolved. Neither experimental dose is a project default. |
| Palmito | UF/IFAS gives general container-palm establishment guidance: regular irrigation for about 6–8 months, often alternate-day water/rain events, adjusted to soil and weather. | Florida landscape guidance, not a *Chamaerops*-specific Almería trial. Its frequency and duration must not be imported as local requirements. It gives no applicable fixed L/application here. | Unresolved, including a locally justified duration. |
| Esparto | A Sierra de Gádor quarry trial planted nursery *Stipa tenacissima* with two shrub species and supplied drip support during the first months, in late spring and summer. | No litres or interval reported in the abstract. All plots received irrigation; it cannot establish the minimum water needed or the benefit relative to no irrigation. | Unresolved. Adult drought adaptation is not proof that a new plug needs no support water. |

Carob source: [Benguerir field experiment, Scientific Reports (2023), Materials and methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10027864/). Site conditions included roughly 150–250 mm annual rainfall and shallow calcareous soil. The study compared delivery regimes; this is not a first-year irrigation prescription.

Palm source: [Broschat, UF/IFAS CIR1047](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/EP001/pdf), PDF p. 3. Its explanation that container substrate can dry differently from adjacent soil is the reason for the poster's root-ball moisture reminder.

Esparto source: [Solé-Benet et al., “Irrigation, organic amendments and mulches as success factors in soil restoration in semiarid limestone quarries”, ESSC 2011 abstract proceedings](https://esdac.jrc.ec.europa.eu/ESDB_Archive/eusoils_docs/Conf/6thESSC.pdf#page=252), PDF p. 252 / printed p. 216. This is a research abstract, with limited method detail.

The Idaho grass guide's short initial landscape-watering schedule and Florida palm schedules belong to their settings. Neither is evidence to stop support water after a fixed number of weeks on an exposed Almería restoration parcel.

## Proposed companion information for a future Planting Explorer

This is a design proposal, not an implemented schedule:

1. **Identify the installation:** actual stock/container, W and H in cm, planting date, microsite, soil texture/depth/drainage and irrigation method.
2. **Set a local water plan:** L/plant/application, observation or soil-moisture trigger, planned interval, review date and intended support period. Show unconfirmed values explicitly. Keep minimum and optimum unknown until their evidence and objectives are defined.
3. **Observe both materials:** check moisture in the original root ball/plug and adjacent ground; verify infiltration and drainage. Adapt delivery to avoid runoff, plug erosion and persistent saturation.
4. **Record response:** rainfall, actual water applied, heat/drought events, plant condition and establishment. Monitoring should cover the first dry season and be reviewed before the next; this is a planning horizon, not proof that irrigation is required continuously throughout it.
5. **Adjust or taper:** confirm rooting into surrounding soil and satisfactory condition as watering is reduced. Do not equate a calendar deadline or one new leaf with established drought independence.

Daily, weekly and monthly are possible scheduling choices, not species traits. Confirm the initial dose and reassessment method with the supplying nursery and site practitioner, then use observations to adjust. This keeps the poster stable while the practical plan remains editable and evidence-linked.
