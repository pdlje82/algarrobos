# Dryland restoration: planting techniques and checklist coverage

Library review, 28 October 2026. Scope: planting and establishment of the demonstrator's plants, using the files currently stored in this repository. No application settings, plant selections or prices were changed.

**Subsequent implementation, 28 October 2026:** the three poster omissions identified below have now been added to the application's current 16-entry catalogue, with names, illustrations and editable unconfirmed prices. The findings below preserve the original 13-entry review snapshot and its source links. This checklist completion does not fill the planting-protocol gaps documented here.

## Main findings

The current checklist contains **13 plant entries**. The dryland vegetation-systems poster contains **16**, including three absent from the checklist: **rosemary (*Salvia rosmarinus*), *Rhamnus lycioides* and *Ephedra fragilis***. These are all in poster system 4. The existing “Buckthorn” entry is *Rhamnus alaternus*, so it does not cover *R. lycioides*. [Dryland poster][POSTER]; [current catalogue][APP-CATALOG]; [plant identity metadata][APP-IDENTITIES].

The strongest practical source is **NIJAR-003**, a plant-supply specification. It documents container formats for **10 of the 13 current entries**, together with provenance, root quality, transport and acceptance requirements. It does **not** supply a complete field-planting protocol for those species. LAUJAR-001 adds soil restoration, broadcast seeding, shrub planting, first watering and replacement planting, but describes disturbed mining-exploration land in a different setting. [NIJAR-003, PDF pp. 1–6][NIJAR-003]; [LAUJAR-001, PDF pp. 69–70, 77–78][LAUJAR-001].

No complete, species-specific combination of planting season, hole dimensions, spacing, irrigation dose and aftercare schedule was found for any checklist entry. The appropriate next step is to obtain those missing protocols for the selected site and nursery stock, while retaining the documented techniques below as evidence. Unknowns should remain explicit; case-study numbers should not become automatic defaults.

## How this review was conducted

The pre-review library contained 27 top-level files: **21 PDFs totalling 1,168 PDF pages**, three PNG posters, one research Markdown file, the catalogue and the rename CSV. One additional archived research Markdown file was also screened. All 28 existing files were included in the screening; the source register below records their relevance.

PDF text was extracted and searched throughout, using plant names, alternative names used in the collection, and Spanish/English terms for planting, sowing, nursery stock, roots, soil preparation, watering, protection, replacement and monitoring. Relevant passages were then read in context. The scanned AL20 tables were rotated in temporary renderings and OCR-screened; the relevant selections were checked visually. The three posters, the Níjar species tables, the Laujar measurements/budget tables and low-text forestry map pages were also inspected visually. This is a full-library screening with focused reading, rather than a claim that every paragraph of every planning document supplies planting guidance.

**Page references always mean the PDF viewer's one-based page number**, including covers. Some documents have different printed page numbers or signature counters. No linked external data package, cited external guideline or web source was treated as a locally reviewed document. This report records the supplied documents; it does not verify present grant availability or current legal requirements.

Evidence is distinguished as follows:

- **Direct specification:** an explicit operation, stock requirement or budget item in a project document. Its parameters belong to that project.
- **Ecological context:** information about vegetation, soil or habitats; useful for site selection, without proving a planting method.
- **Illustration/learning synthesis:** a conceptual guide, without the authority or detail of a planting specification.
- **Planning inference:** a proposed use of the evidence, labelled as such.
- **Gap:** information not located in this collection, rather than evidence that a technique is unsuitable.

## 2. Documented techniques and operations

### 2.1 Choose the microsite and retain existing vegetation

**Ecological context.** The poster distinguishes seasonal watercourses; deeper, sheltered woodland–shrub sites; dry shrub/grass slopes; and very arid exposed scrub. Its water icons are qualitative. They are not irrigation quantities, survival probabilities or measured soil thresholds. Its author and original publication source are unverified. [Poster][POSTER].

The larger planning documents support treating these habitats separately:

- Esparto on dry slopes and moisture-associated tamarisk vegetation are described as different communities. [VEGA-001, PDF p. 37][VEGA-001]; [VEGA-003, PDF p. 88][VEGA-003].
- Seasonal-stream oleander/tamarisk formations vary with flooding, salinity and substrate. A generic *Tamarix* selection leaves a real ecological decision unresolved. [VEGA-003, PDF p. 147][VEGA-003].
- The *Ziziphus*-associated habitat account describes warm, low-altitude, frost-free semi-arid settings. This is habitat context, without a planting-success trial for the demonstrator. The source spells *Lycium* as “intrincatum” in this passage; the checklist and supply specification use *L. intricatum*. [VEGA-003, PDF p. 151][VEGA-003].

**Direct project measures.** Laujar minimises new access and disturbance, restores closed access routes and positions platforms to avoid existing trees. Vega calls for pre-work flora surveys, defined access/storage areas and protection of retained vegetation from machinery, stockpiles and contamination. These provide evidence for marking planting areas and retained vegetation **before** soil work. [LAUJAR-001, PDF pp. 64–65][LAUJAR-001]; [VEGA-003, PDF pp. 254–255][VEGA-003].

**Planning inference:** first map the parcel's existing plants, exposed soil, slope/aspect, rooting conditions and water setting. Then choose planting patches. Combining checklist mixes need not imply uniform spacing or identical soil treatment across the parcel.

### 2.2 Source appropriate nursery plants and keep provenance traceable

**Direct specification.** Níjar requires an accredited nursery and plant/seed origins from ecologically comparable areas. It specifies the Murciano-Almeriense context and provenance region 38; for relevant shrubs without a formally established provenance region it describes district-level origin. Documentation includes origin certification/labelling and a phytosanitary passport. Its *Populus* exception is a project-specific provision. [NIJAR-003, PDF pp. 2–3, 6][NIJAR-003].

The transferable practice is to record the **exact taxon, nursery, propagation material origin, batch and documentation**, rather than accepting “native plant” as the whole specification. The particular provenance numbers and legal references are those in the supplied contract; they are not a universal rule or a verified current requirement for this finca.

### 2.3 Inspect roots, root ball, container and plant condition

**Direct specification.** Níjar provides a useful nursery acceptance checklist:

- A healthy plant with appropriate natural form and balance between shoot and roots; inspect for disease, pests, wounds, damaged root collars, dehydration and poor storage/transport condition.
- Numerous, well-distributed secondary roots. Reject the strongly spiralled, twisted or upward-rebounding root systems described in the specification.
- Forestry containers with ribs, grooves or other anti-spiralling features; container walls prevent roots growing into neighbouring containers.
- A root ball that occupies the container and remains cohesive when removed, with substrate relatively moist and not excessively compacted.
- Nursery containers raised at least **10 cm** above ground to allow air pruning; this is a **nursery production requirement**, not the height at which a plant is installed in the field.

Source: [NIJAR-003, PDF pp. 4–6][NIJAR-003].

The specification also gives nursery-age classes of one or two growing seasons, a generic basal-stem diameter range of **4–6 mm**, and peat/peat-mix substrate with **60–80% porosity**. The diameter clause refers to a height interval not supplied alongside it; it should not be converted into a universal acceptance threshold for all checklist plants. The substrate clause describes the supplied root ball, **not an instruction to fill finca planting holes with peat**. [NIJAR-003, PDF pp. 4–5][NIJAR-003].

There are generic single-stem/terminal-leader clauses alongside references to natural form and shrub exceptions. Their application needs species and growth-form review, particularly for palmetto and naturally branching shrubs. The document also discusses 1.6 L containers generically, although that size is not assigned to a species in its order tables. Preserve those distinctions rather than silently inventing missing stock specifications.

### 2.4 Transport and receive plants without deterioration

**Direct specification.** Níjar requires rapid transport with precautions against plant damage, phytosanitary documentation, inspection at receipt and rejection of defective stock. It explicitly lists desiccation, damaged roots and signs of overheating/fermentation among rejection concerns. [NIJAR-003, PDF pp. 4, 6][NIJAR-003].

**Planning inference:** coordinate delivery with planting readiness and record accepted/rejected batches. No maximum storage time, holding-area design or watering schedule while awaiting planting was found; obtain those instructions from the nursery rather than assigning an unsupported duration.

### 2.5 Restore disturbed ground and relieve compaction where justified

**Direct case method.** Laujar restores landform and soil after exploration works, reuses salvaged topsoil, and warns against heavy machinery compacting wet ground. At layer contacts it specifies **5–10 cm scarification**, or **20–30 cm** where the underlying material is compacted, to improve contact, infiltration and root penetration. Slope is also identified as relevant to infiltration. [LAUJAR-001, PDF pp. 69–70][LAUJAR-001].

The same case includes topsoil/aggregate layers and a topsoil/quarry-fines mixture. These address reconstructed exploration platforms and access tracks. They are **not a blanket preparation recipe for an intact finca**. The source also conflicts internally about layer thicknesses and quantities; see section 3.

The library contains a useful caution against indiscriminate disturbance: Laujar's baseline describes earlier pine establishment involving scrub clearance and terracing that damaged soil horizons and left a poorly developed shrub layer. This is a description of an adverse historical intervention, not a technique to copy. [LAUJAR-001, PDF p. 42][LAUJAR-001].

**Gap:** no site-specific compaction diagnosis, planting-hole dimensions, root-collar placement instructions or treatment of intact soil crusts was found for the demonstrator's plants. Scarification depth and container volume do not answer those questions.

### 2.6 Plant nursery stock, with installation separate from supply

**Direct case method.** Laujar specifies native shrubs supplied in forestry trays and includes digging/backfilling holes, fertilising, first watering, establishment care and auxiliary resources in its installation budget item. Its nearby shrub examples are “tomillo, romero, espliego, jara, jaguarzo, estepa”; these common names do not constitute a fully resolved species mix. [LAUJAR-001, PDF pp. 77–78][LAUJAR-001].

This supports distinguishing **buying plants** from **installing and establishing them**. It does not specify hole width/depth, fertiliser formulation/dose, planting date, handling of individual root systems or water volume. Fertilisation is included in this case's item; it is not evidence that every dryland plant or soil needs fertiliser.

The budget labels item PR202 “siembra”, although its hole-digging/backfilling description and the preceding forestry-tray stock indicate a planting operation. Treat the actual operation as the evidence; do not turn this wording into a seed-propagation protocol.

### 2.7 Broadcast seeding as a separate intervention

**Direct case method.** Laujar also describes mixed herbaceous/shrub broadcast seeding, selected according to the local vegetation series, at **125 kg/ha**, with two passes for an even distribution. Its budget measures this separately by area. [LAUJAR-001, PDF pp. 70, 77–78][LAUJAR-001].

This provides evidence for an area-based sowing activity alongside individually counted nursery plants. It supplies neither a named seed-mixture composition nor species-specific germination, pretreatment or establishment results. Do not apply the mixed-seed rate to retama, carob, esparto or another checklist species as an individual sowing prescription. No verified direct-seeding recipe, cuttings protocol or division protocol for the checklist entries was located.

### 2.8 Establishment water, care and replacement planting

**Direct evidence.** Laujar includes first watering and subsequent establishment care, plus a separate **20% replacement allowance**. Níjar's works description explicitly includes replacement of losses from earlier planting pilots. These are evidence that planting is followed by care and correction; neither gives the survival result of the demonstrator. [LAUJAR-001, PDF p. 78][LAUJAR-001]; [NIJAR-001, PDF p. 5][NIJAR-001].

The Vega environmental assessment contains irrigation assumptions for a **network of parks**: its water-demand table includes 48 annual irrigation events and an aggregate annual demand. These are landscape planning assumptions across specified park areas, not per-plant doses or schedules for the 13 checklist entries. The associated water-allocation discussion also does not establish access to water for this project. [VEGA-003, PDF pp. 208–209, 215–216, 270][VEGA-003].

**Gaps:** watering volume and frequency, soil-moisture triggers, drought response, care duration, browsing protection design, and a justified replacement strategy for each planting patch. The 20% case allowance must not be interpreted as a measured mortality rate or a prediction of 80% survival.

### 2.9 Monitor establishment and adjust the intervention

**Direct project requirements.** Níjar's supervision document calls for checking implementation and the effectiveness of environmental measures, with corrective action when needed. COAST-001 requires conservation/biodiversity reporting before and after work and photographic evidence. COAST-002 includes restored area, vegetation/planting and ecological indicators in its action framework. [NIJAR-001, PDF pp. 6–7][NIJAR-001]; [COAST-001, PDF pp. 8–9][COAST-001]; [COAST-002, PDF pp. 26–27][COAST-002].

**Learning synthesis.** LEARN-001 proposes a small, measured pilot, baseline photographs and observations of vegetation, exposed soil, erosion and rainfall. It frames successful dryland restoration as a functioning mosaic, including shrubs/herbs and soil processes, rather than simply maximising the number of trees. This is an orientation document, not a controlled species-establishment study. [LEARN-001, PDF pp. 1–2][LEARN-001].

**Planning inference:** record planted and surviving individuals by species and planting date, distinguish original plants from replacements, retain repeat photo points, and relate outcomes to microsite, soil work and actual watering. Where a technique is compared, record both the treatment and a comparable reference area. No validated monitoring interval or survival target for this demonstrator was found.

### 2.10 Existing-stand management is a different operation

FOREST-001 specifies thinning a dense existing pine stand, followed by wood extraction, slash gathering and mechanical shredding, to improve habitat conditions. Its historical account mentions manual pits, ox-drawn ridges and regular planting patterns used in past reforestation. [FOREST-001, PDF pp. 6, 13–14, 18–20][FOREST-001].

This is relevant when considering how existing vegetation affects habitat recovery. It is **not a new-planting protocol for the checklist**, and mechanical treatment of forestry residues does not establish a mulch material, mulch depth or mulch placement around young plants. Nor do the historical pine methods justify adopting terracing or a regular grid on the finca.

## 4. What the funding documents contribute

The AL20 tables include **3.4 demonstration activities** and **3.5 conservation/recovery of rural heritage including environmental heritage** among the selected operations. They classify activities; they do not prescribe planting holes, plant mixtures or irrigation. The 3.4 selection was checked visually in FUND-004 p. 15 and FUND-005 p. 1. [FUND-004][FUND-004]; [FUND-005][FUND-005].

FUND-001 separately distinguishes planting material, planting operations, related protective measures and replacement costs, and discusses ecological suitability and biodiversity constraints. It is a different grant framework, not the AL20 planting specification or proof that a particular expense is eligible for this project. Its replacement percentage provisions should not be confused with Laujar's budget allowance. [FUND-001, PDF pp. 14–16][FUND-001].

FUND-003 provides financing and stewardship context. FUND-006 includes research, restoration and living-lab/monitoring themes, but no operational protocol for the checklist plants was located. Neither replaces the missing field instructions. [FUND-003][FUND-003]; [FUND-006, including PDF pp. 140–141][FUND-006].

**Planning inference for a demonstrator:** keep the planting method, learning question, observations and public demonstration outputs identifiable. Select a funding category from the actual activity and its applicable conditions, rather than treating a plant or technique as automatically “fundable”. Current eligibility and approvals require a separate check when preparing an application.
