A Small Trial Before a Park
A researched proposal for Cortijo Conjuro, prepared on the third of November, twenty twenty-six

Chapter one. The recommendation

Develop a small, publicly accessible native-vegetation learning demonstrator on a suitable part of your own land at Cortijo Conjuro. Use it to investigate establishment, water needs and participation before deciding whether to pursue a larger educational restoration park on the neighbouring parcel. The neighbouring park remains an option, rather than a commitment.

The recommended starting design reserves up to two hundred square metres for twelve young plants arranged in six matched pairs. It compares added stone mulch with no added stone. It includes a baseline survey, twelve months of observations, four public learning sessions and an openly available account of the methods and results.

An existing environmental nonprofit with relevant competence is the preferred applicant to investigate. Your role would be landowner and host under a written agreement. No organisation has agreed to apply, and neither the field design nor the funding case is approved.

The funding conclusion is important. The saved draft does not establish that the project meets the selection threshold. After withdrawing three claims that the proposed restoration trial does not currently justify, the provisional subtotal is forty-eight points, against a minimum of sixty. Several remaining points also depend on evidence and future delivery. This proposal therefore provides a coherent project to discuss with the rural development group, together with a clear account of what prevents a defensible funding application today.

Chapter two. What the saved project actually contains

The starting point is the live working draft called Al twenty Alhama, in the project My restoration journey. The live app and saved file matched when checked. There were no separately saved funding alternatives or adopted funding plans in that workspace.

The retained funding snapshot was checked at the end of October, twenty twenty-six. It uses the older scoring edition in which a criterion must reach its cap or have all applicable answers completed. The assessment retains that edition. No saved inputs or rules have been changed.

The app contains a two-hundred-square-metre planting plan with twenty plants: four carobs, four lentisks, two kermes oaks, two spiny buckthorns, one holm oak, three thyme plants and four retamas. Its illustrative total is seven hundred and forty-seven euros and fifty cents. Nursery prices are marked unconfirmed, the water plan is unknown, and several practical and ecological questions remain unanswered.

Your clarification changes how the placeholders should be understood. Cortijo Conjuro is your home. The neighbour's dryland parcel is real, although its future availability is undecided. The nonprofit entry represents a possible applicant arrangement, rather than an identified organisation with a confirmed commitment.

Public cadastral data locate both parcels in the Conjuro area of Alhama de Almeria. The mapped parcel containing your land and house has a stated area of four thousand four hundred and seventy-nine square metres. The neighbouring parcel has three thousand and fifty-six square metres. These are cadastral records, not a survey of usable planting space or proof of title. The app's four-thousand-five-hundred-square-metre input remains unchanged.

Chapter three. Who could apply, and how your land could be used

For these two funding categories, the governing rules list public or private nonprofit legal entities, public-law bodies and certain publicly participated companies. They do not provide an ordinary individual-applicant route for this proposal. Owning the land, or offering public access, does not by itself change that restriction.

Your suggested environmental contacts provide a sensible starting point. The Grupo Ecologista Andarax and its Plantabosques activities have published accounts of native planting and maintenance in Alhama. Barjali is described as a mountain-sports section associated with that group. These names should not be treated as three automatically independent legal applicants. Their current legal identities, statutes, capacity, memberships and grant history need documents.

Kalima du Samba has independently reported cultural and community activity in the territory. That could make it a useful participation contact. It does not establish ecological competence or grant eligibility for a restoration demonstrator. A newly founded association is another possibility, but it would need appropriate legal status and relevant competence when applying. None of these options is a confirmed partner.

The preferred arrangement to explore is a competent environmental nonprofit managing a trial on your land. The agreement should identify the trial area, permitted work, access for participants and inspections, water responsibility, maintenance, publication duties, liability and arrangements if either party withdraws. The nonprofit must actually govern the project and account for the expenditure.

Scheduled, openly advertised visits could protect household privacy while supporting public participation. Access only for invited public bodies would need to be expanded or clarified against the applicable participation requirements. The house and domestic improvements are outside this proposal.

Chapter four. Establish what should be restored

The neighbour's parcel is reported to be unused former grape-growing land with abundant salsola plants. That history and vegetation description come from you. They have not been confirmed by a field survey. The cadastral records classify the agricultural components as arable land, which is an administrative description rather than a present ecological diagnosis.

Before choosing plants or clearing ground, a competent field assessment should identify the existing species, soil and microsites. It should check effective rooting depth, texture, salinity, infiltration, slopes, runoff routes, existing native cover and possible protected features. Salsola should be identified to species and assessed for its role in the present vegetation. Abandonment does not by itself demonstrate that the site needs more trees.

The own-land option is recommended because it avoids making the first trial depend on a neighbour's sale or usufruct decision. It still requires a suitable patch, a documented use agreement and the relevant permission checks. The two-hundred-square-metre ceiling comes from your saved plan. It is not a surveyed allocation or proof that twelve plants will fit appropriately.

The site review must distinguish cadastral agricultural use from any environmental or forestry classification, and check municipal requirements, habitat constraints, water arrangements and any earthworks permissions. A geographic screening is only part of this work. No permission clearance is inferred from a cadastral reference or a map.

If the own-land patch is unsuitable, the neighbour's land can be reconsidered after a voluntary agreement and a fresh site assessment. The project must not quietly move to that parcel or assume future access.

Chapter five. What would make this a demonstrator

Local native planting is already taking place. Published Plantabosques accounts describe planting, watering and maintenance in Alhama in twenty twenty-three and twenty twenty-four. The regional agricultural institute has also run learning activities involving native aromatic plants and a nursery in Alhama. The proposal should therefore avoid claiming to introduce native planting or environmental learning to the territory for the first time.

The candidate demonstration question is more specific. Under a documented, limited external-water provision, does adding stone mulch change the practical establishment performance of selected native plants, and can residents understand and repeat the observation method?

Stone mulch itself is established guidance. An Andalusian carob handbook discusses it in restoration and dryland planting. The possible local novelty lies in a particular, transparent comparison and its combined learning method. This remains a hypothesis about the funding fit. The rural development group, local practitioners and technical advisers should check whether the approach is actually new or unfamiliar in the territory.

Before applying, prepare a short inventory of comparable local trials, their methods and their available results. Ask the environmental groups what they have already tested. If the proposed comparison duplicates established local knowledge, revise the question openly or accept that category three point four may not fit.

The trial must stand on its own. It will deliver observations, a public method and an honest interpretation even if the park never happens or the mulch shows no benefit. Preparing a future park is a secondary use of those results, rather than a condition for their usefulness.

Chapter six. A feasible comparison, with limits

The proposed plant list is two seed-grown carobs, four lentisks and six retamas, with retama's precise species identity confirmed before buying. These candidates are drawn from the saved selection and regional restoration evidence. Their suitability at the actual microsites, and the provenance and quality of nursery stock, still require confirmation.

This is a proposed change from the twenty-plant saved mix. The holm oak, kermes oak, spiny buckthorn and thyme are held outside the first experiment, and the other quantities are revised. The purpose is to simplify the comparison and reduce initial planting commitments. It is not a finding that the omitted species are unsuitable for the wider project.

Create six same-species pairs: one carob pair, two lentisk pairs and three retama pairs. Match each pair as closely as practicable for soil, exposure and nursery stock. Randomly assign one member to added stone mulch and the other to no added stone. Keep pre-existing natural cover in both. Do not strip stones or vegetation to manufacture an artificial control.

Planting preparation, guards and water rules should otherwise be comparable within each pair. Do not add a second experimental treatment, such as different fertilisers or runoff structures, without redesigning the protocol. The ecologist should approve safe preparation and mulch dimensions. The app's drawing dimensions are illustrations, rather than field prescriptions.

Six pairs are a small feasibility trial. Differences in species representation and local microsites limit interpretation. Report the observations for each pair and species, including losses and exceptions. Do not treat monthly observations as independent experimental replicates or claim a general species ranking. Mark a small undisturbed reference area to observe background change, while recognising that it is not an equivalent planted control.

Chapter seven. Water, monitoring and decisions

Reserve a provisional first-year supply capacity of six hundred litres for establishment and rescue watering. Across twelve plants, that is an average capacity of fifty litres per plant, not a prescribed dose, a fixed schedule or a prediction of survival. The supply method, source, actual cost and appropriate watering triggers remain to be agreed.

The ecologist should set establishment and rescue rules before planting, based on the stock and field conditions. Within a matched pair, apply comparable rules and record departures. Log litres for each plant, dates, rainfall observations, labour and reasons for rescue watering. If the proposed capacity is inadequate, revise the plan visibly. Do not let plants die simply to protect an arbitrary budget.

At baseline, identify every plant, record its condition, photograph its position and document the treatment. Then make twelve monthly monitoring visits, with additional checks during critical dry periods where needed. Record survival, condition, a consistent growth measure, water supplied, maintenance time and visible soil disturbance. A simple moisture observation may support interpretation, but it is not a certified water-footprint assessment.

The proposed operational target is at least ninety percent completion of the planned plant records. A provisional management target is at least ten of the twelve original plants alive at twelve months, without unacceptable habitat damage and with transparent water records. These are author-proposed decision thresholds, not published ecological standards, expected results or grant requirements. They need technical and owner review before use.

Report replacements separately, rather than counting them as surviving original plants. If a method fails, explain why it should be revised. One year of establishment observations cannot demonstrate a mature forest, long-term drought resilience or permanent carbon storage.

Chapter eight. Make the public value deliverable

The proposed programme has four learning sessions, with a planning target of twelve places per session. The first develops the learning question and participation arrangements. The second demonstrates planting, measurement and treatment allocation. The third discusses the dry-season observations. The fourth presents the results and tests what participants understood.

Those forty-eight places are planned attendance opportunities, not forty-eight confirmed or unique participants. Safe capacity and access need checking. Recruitment should be open and transparent, with arrangements for women, young people and people at risk of exclusion based on actual needs and appropriate support.

The saved selections include women's participation, youth activities and trained support for vulnerable people. Translate them into responsibilities, accessible materials, facilitation and evidence of delivery. A named person in a saved note is not proof of appointment, qualifications or availability. Nor does attending a planting day automatically satisfy every social criterion.

The prospective nonprofit would be responsible for governance, expenditure and publication. An appointed ecologist would lead technical design and interpretation. A named observer would maintain field records, and a suitably qualified facilitator would coordinate participation. You would provide the agreed landowner and host functions. These are proposed roles awaiting consent and capacity checks.

Publish the method from the start and add results as they emerge. Keep the required information available for at least three years after final payment. Include an accessible summary, a practical learning sheet and an anonymised dataset where appropriate. Publication and maintenance duties need a named responsible organisation and resources beyond the trial itself.

Chapter nine. Schedule, budget and cash

Use a schedule relative to approvals and the planting window, rather than promising an immediate start. First resolve the applicant, land agreement, novelty and scoring questions. Then complete the permissions and funding steps, including any required non-start inspection, before beginning grant-dependent works or making commitments that could jeopardise eligibility.

After those gates, allow preparation and baseline work, plant in a technically suitable moisture window, and observe for twelve months. Plan analysis and the final public session around months thirteen and fourteen. A final payment at month fifteen is used only for the cash-flow example. The grant decision must establish the actual execution and justification deadlines.

The rules allow up to one hundred percent of accepted eligible costs, capped at twenty-eight thousand euros for this demonstration category. That does not establish an award or mean every proposed cost is eligible. The published application window currently runs to the end of twenty twenty-eight, subject to local interruptions and the applicable allocation being available.

The working budget is eight thousand seven hundred and forty-five euros. It is an estimate assembled from explicit allowances, not quotations. It includes two thousand eight hundred euros for the nonprofit's own attributable project staff, two thousand one hundred for ecological work, two hundred and seventy for soil analyses, and one thousand and eighty for monthly field visits. Participation facilitation has one thousand euros, and report and dataset preparation has four hundred and eighty. The remainder covers plants, protection, stone, limited tool hire, water, event arrangements, materials and initial hosting.

The personnel allowance assumes eighty attributable hours at a provisional loaded cost of thirty-five euros per hour within an eligible period. It is not a commercial fee or confirmed payroll. Supplier figures are planning ceilings. Recoverable value-added tax must be excluded from any later eligible claim. The proposed operation also needs a documented check of subcontracting limits. The rules generally require at least three comparable supplier offers before contracting, with any justified exception properly documented. A small budget should not be treated as an automatic exemption from that requirement.

Separate private reserves cover a ten-percent contingency, later coordination and publication hosting. Without an advance, the illustrative total liquidity requirement is eleven thousand three hundred and forty-four euros and fifty cents. The app's simulated cash is not evidence that this money is available. An advance may be possible under the rules, but approval, guarantees and their costs cannot be assumed. Do not commit the project until the actual financing is evidenced.

Chapter ten. What the scoring says

The saved assessment has a subtotal of fifty-seven point four nine, but no completed total because two criteria remain unresolved. Supporting references and ticked boxes are self-assessment inputs, rather than certified evidence.

The recommended review withdraws three claims from the proposed funding case. The trial does not currently include qualifying renewable-energy equipment. Measuring soil moisture does not establish precision agriculture under the strategy's specified innovation aspect. Ordinary restoration activity does not by itself demonstrate conservation of a traditional craft or occupation.

Removing those claims leaves a provisional subtotal of forty-eight. This still assumes delivery and acceptance of the remaining selected claims, including relevant heritage work, social activities, qualifying membership and applicant history. If the membership and never-funded claims are also unsupported, the corresponding model is thirty-nine points. Neither figure establishes eligibility.

An explicitly hypothetical model reaches fifty-seven if a genuinely new local service and a second qualifying membership are both accepted. Adding a legitimately qualifying settlement-population award could bring that model to sixty-five. None of these additions is confirmed. In particular, living outside the town centre does not let us invent a settlement population, and an association is not automatically a qualifying member of itself.

The practical recommendation is to ask the rural development group to screen the real applicant, settlement and activity before spending money on an elaborate application. If sixty defensible points cannot be established without distorting the project, keep the useful trial concept and reconsider its financing. A smaller privately financed exercise would be a separate decision, with separate costs, rather than a way to claim retrospective grant support.

Chapter eleven. What the trial would tell us about a future park

The possible park on the neighbour's parcel remains an educational and ecological idea. No sale, usufruct or other access agreement has been reached. The first trial should not require you to buy that land or commit to a permanent park.

Use the trial to identify establishment problems, water demand, maintenance tasks and educational activities that people find useful. Its small sample can guide the next investigation, but it cannot justify multiplying twelve plants across the whole neighbouring parcel or converting all existing salsola vegetation into tree planting.

For a later category three point five proposal, identify the environmental heritage resource and demonstrate the conservation or recovery benefit. Prepare the appropriate site design, land rights, management arrangements and public-use or dissemination plan. Separate permanent restoration expenditure from the demonstrator's costs, and prevent duplicate financing. Reassess the applicant and scoring then; an earlier grant may change its previous-beneficiary history.

Before advancing the funding case, obtain answers to five central questions. Who is the eligible and competent applicant? What exact land rights and permissions support the operation? Is the comparison locally novel enough for category three point four? Which selection claims will the rural development group accept with specific evidence? And who can finance delivery, reimbursement delays and continuing publication?

The recommended next decision is to review this small demonstrator with a suitable environmental association and the rural development group. It offers a practical way to learn before making a larger land or park commitment. The research has produced a proposal and an evidence trail. It has not produced field results, a partner commitment, a permission or a funding award.
